SVG charts

OffPrint draws line, bar, scatter, and pie charts that work offline, fit the reading column, and move smoothly when their data changes. Bars can stand upright, lie sideways, or stack, and readers can always see exact values in a table.

Turn bars sideways or stack them

Bars can lie sideways, which leaves room for long category names. Several series can sit side by side, stack into one bar per category, or stack to 100% so each bar shows shares instead of totals.

Layout

Try it

Choose Percent and watch each bar stretch to the full width. Then choose Upright and Side by side. Hide Script in the legend to see the stylesheet bars restack on their own.

Percent bars suit a question about shares, such as how much of each feature is styling. Totals answer a question about size. A percent chart cannot include negative values, so OffPrint rejects those rather than guessing.

Charts that move with their data

Every chart animates by default. Each chart draws itself in once, the first time you scroll it into view: bars grow, lines draw, and dots pop in. After that, any change moves the existing marks to their new places instead of redrawing. That includes a filter, a hidden series, a new bin count, or a new layout. Your eye can follow each bar or dot from where it was to where it is now.

Try it

Switch the bar layout above a few times and follow one bar, such as Parameters, as it moves.

Movement is skipped when your device asks for reduced motion, when you print, and inside the full-screen zoom view. Charts then show their final state at once. Authors can also turn motion off for one chart or for a whole site.

Show shares with a pie chart

Choose a group in the legend to hide or show its slice. The pie and table recalculate each share using the total of the visible groups.

Compare file sizes with a scatter chart

A scatter chart puts one dot at the intersection of two numbers. Here, each dot uses the size and line count of a repository file, and the series group the records by kind. A linear axis adds the same amount over equal distances. The horizontal log scale here multiplies by ten at each major step and takes only positive values.

Show uncertainty with intervals

Try it

Inspect the middle point and switch to Table to read the estimate, bounds, and count.

Bars and scatter points show supplied intervals as whiskers. A line chart can also use a whisker where only one point has bounds.

Rank categories with dot and interval plots

A dot plot ranks categories by their estimates. When you supply bounds, whiskers show the range around each dot. You can put categories down the left or along the bottom, and add stems to make a lollipop plot.

Hide First read in the legend to see the remaining dots move to the middle of each row. Choose Table to see the remaining estimates and bounds.

See how values are distributed

A histogram counts how many readings fall in each bin. Move the slider to see how wider or narrower bins change the shape. Use the legend to show either group on its own.

Number of bins: 4

Choose Table to see the exact bin boundaries and counts. A logarithmic horizontal axis can help when positive measurements span several orders of magnitude. The slider is linked straight to the chart, so it needs no extra page code. Parameters explains these links.

Colour a grid of values

When values sit on a grid, such as two settings crossed with each other, a heatmap colours each cell by its value and can draw contour lines through equal values. Heatmaps has its own page.

Point things out with chart annotations

Chart annotations add a note to the chart itself, so the reader sees what happened where it happened. A note points at a value with a short line, a range shades a stretch of the chart, and a marker draws a labelled line at one moment. Notes find a free spot on their own, away from the data and from each other.

  1. Before the release Requests run steadily, apart from a planned maintenance window.
  2. The spike The new release ships and waiting time climbs.
  3. Recovery Clearing the cache brings waiting time back down.

Try it

Step through the list to reveal each annotation. Then drag across hours 1 to 5 to narrow the chart: annotations outside the range disappear, and the legend says how many are hidden. Choose Table to see every annotation listed under the values.

Annotations stay in place when the chart changes size, follow its data when it updates, and work on every chart type with axes, including bars, interval plots, histograms, and heatmaps. A long note is shortened on the chart, and the full text appears when you point at it. Any step sequence can reveal annotations, such as a worked example, figure steps, or a guided playback tour. In an exported Reader copy, they come with their own step buttons.

Combine bars and a line, with a second axis

A combo chart draws some series as bars and others as a line over the same categories or dates. When the line measures something different, such as a rate next to a count, it can read on a second axis at the right. That axis is off unless a series asks for it, both axes start at zero, and each axis takes the colour of its series. If the two axes share a unit, or one has none, OffPrint warns the author: a second axis then invites the reader to compare heights that mean different things.

Try it

Point at April to read every series, each with the side of its axis. Choose Side by side to see the bars move apart, then hide Failed in the legend. Choose Table to see which axis each column reads on.

Use a second axis only for a different kind of measure. Two measures in the same unit belong on one axis, where their heights can be compared directly. On a date axis, a combo chart can also be narrowed by dragging across it, like a line chart.

Share a position between charts

Charts with a common linear horizontal scale can share an inspection cursor while keeping their own vertical scales and visible ranges. Try the two charts in Guided chart playback.

When to reach for it

Use a line to show change in order, bars to compare separate categories, and dots to see how two measurements relate. Lay bars sideways when category names are long, and stack them only when the total means something. Give the chart room and keep the nearby prose short.

For every reader

Tab to the plot and use the arrow keys or Home and End to inspect values. In a chart with a range brush, + and − change the range and Shift with the left or right arrow pans it. Use Table for exact values on a small screen or in print. The charts follow the page theme, and static tables give readers values with scripts off.
