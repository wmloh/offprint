Fits and residuals

A fit curve shows the trend a model found in your data, drawn over the points it was fitted to. A shaded band can show its uncertainty, and a small panel under the plot shows each residual: how far each point sits from its fitted value.

See it

Try it

Move over a dot in the panel under the plot. The readout names the same sample as the dot above it, with its fitted value and residual. Click a dot to select it, and its partner in the other half of the chart is selected too. Then click the Straight-line fit chip in the legend to hide the fit and its residuals.

Supplied, not calculated

OffPrint never fits a model for you. You work out the fit in your analysis tool and write three things into the chart: the points of the curve, optionally the lower and upper edges of its band, and a short line of statistics such as R² or the equation. Each observed point can also carry its fitted value. The page joins the curve points you give it with straight segments, so give enough points for a smooth curve.

This keeps the page honest. The numbers a reader sees are the numbers your analysis produced, and nothing is quietly re-estimated in the browser.

Read the residual panel

Each dot in the panel is one observation minus its fitted value. Dots above the zero line are readings the fit underestimates; dots below are readings it overestimates. When the dots scatter evenly around zero, with no curve or funnel shape, the fit suits the data. The panel shares the plot’s x axis, so narrowing the range or reading a point works across both.

Compare fits

A chart can carry several fits, each belonging to one series and drawn in its colour. On a line chart the fit is dashed, so it stays apart from the measured line.

Try it

Hide Cell B with its series chip, and its fit goes with it. Hide only Cell A decay to compare the raw line with nothing drawn over it.

From a data file

Fits can come from the same named data source as the points. Name the columns that hold the fitted values and the band edges, and OffPrint builds the curve and band from them, skipping rows where those columns are blank. A log axis needs every curve, band, and fitted value above zero; OffPrint reports a value of zero or less instead of hiding it.

When to reach for it

Use a fit when the model is part of the story: a calibration line, a growth or decay curve, or a trend you will refer back to. Add the residual panel when readers should judge how well it fits. A fit is a strong signal, so explain in the text what model it is and where its numbers came from, and keep one or two fits per chart.

For every reader

The arrow keys move between points and the readout adds each point’s fitted value and residual. The fit chips work with the keyboard like other legend chips. Choose Table to see the fit statistics and a list of every residual. With scripts off, the written table stays readable.
