Compare words in a concept table

A concept table gives the first column extra room for row labels. Light rules and subdued headings keep attention on the words. Move over a cell to see its row and column together.

Try it

Choose the References heading and watch the rows slide into order. Choose it again to reverse the order, then a third time to restore the starting order. Look for both groups tied for the best count.

A heatmap uses tint strength to show magnitude. Here, the fixed scale runs from zero to 15, so a count keeps its shade even when you filter the rows. A relative scale adjusts to the visible values. The tint colour is also configurable. The threshold gives every qualifying number a Pass label and every lower number a Fail label.

Narrow the rows

The filter button beside each heading opens choices for that column. Group offers a checklist and References offers minimum and maximum values. A date column can offer a from and to range. A filter chip applies a named rule in one press. The table shows rows that meet every active rule and reports how many are visible.

Try it

Turn on At least nine to fade out the rows below the cutoff, then set the References maximum to 12. The data group is the only match. Choose Clear to restore all the rows.

When to reach for it

Choose a concept table when readers compare descriptions and a results table when they need to order, narrow, or judge measurements. Reserve shading for a column readers should scan first, and keep its numbers visible.

For every reader

Tab reaches the sort buttons, filter buttons, and chips. Rows keep their text labels in light and dark themes. On a phone, scroll a wide table sideways. For print, clear filters if you want all rows. With scripts off, both tables still show their starting values.
