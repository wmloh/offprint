Pipeline diagrams

A pipeline diagram shows the stages of a process as blocks joined by lines. Point at a block to trace where it comes from and where it leads, and read more about it in a detail pane.

See it

Try it

Move your pointer over Terms. The lines and blocks before and after it stay bright while the rest fade, and the pane beside the diagram explains it. Tab into the diagram and use the arrow keys to follow the lines.

The Terms block has an empty eyebrow, so it simply leaves that line out. Blocks in the same column always share one height so they line up.

Reveal a pipeline in steps

A pipeline and a worked-example stepper move together when they share a selection group and use matching step keys.

Adjust a block by hand

Blocks are placed automatically, but you can pin one to a column or row, change its size, or give it a different look. A block with its own look still takes its words from the eyebrow, headline, and description, so editing those updates the picture too. When two lines have to cross, the line underneath gets a small gap.

Lines can be routed by hand as well. A line can leave and enter a block on a chosen side, bend through a point between the blocks, carry arrowheads at both ends, or use a dotted or dashed style.

Detail panes elsewhere on the page

The detail pane is not tied to a diagram. Place one anywhere and point it at a pipeline, a card panel, or a figure with steps. It shows the detail whose name matches the selected item.

When to reach for it

Use a pipeline diagram when order and connections matter: stages that split, run side by side, and come back together. For a single list of choices without connections, a card panel is simpler.

For every reader

Point at, tap, or focus a block to select it. Arrow keys follow the lines forward and back, or move between blocks in the same column. Each block also says where it leads for screen readers. With scripts off, or when printed, the diagram becomes a numbered list and every detail is shown.
