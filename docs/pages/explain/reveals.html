Scroll reveals

Scroll reveals let blocks glide or fade in as they near the middle of the screen, and again each time you scroll back to them. Printed pages and pages without scripts still show every block.

How it works

A marked block waits just out of place, faded and slightly shifted. When it moves into the middle part of the screen it glides into position. When you scroll it well away from the middle, above or below, it quietly steps back out, ready to play again the next time it returns.

Blocks near the very top or bottom of a page can still play, because the middle band stretches to reach the page ends. A block you are typing in or have tabbed into stays put.

Add a pause or keep a block still

A scroll reveal waits until a block nears the middle of the screen. The block can rise, enter from either side, or fade in place. A delay waits a little longer after the block reaches that point. In the first demo, the delays are 0, 160, 320 and 480 milliseconds.

Try it

Scroll slowly into the second example. The first line is readable as soon as you reach it.

When to reach for it

Use a short entrance to lead the eye from one idea to the next. Mark a block to play only once when repeated motion would distract, such as a key result readers return to. Keep core instructions stationary so fast readers and people jumping between headings can start reading straight away. Fade in place when sideways movement would compete with the text.

For every reader

Reveals follow scrolling, including when you move through the page with the keyboard. The same content stays readable at narrow widths and in either theme. Print and pages opened with scripts off show every block immediately, including blocks you have yet to scroll to.
