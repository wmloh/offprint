Tree diagrams

A tree diagram shows how a topic, plan, or set of categories breaks into smaller parts, and readers can close branches to focus on one part at a time.

See it

Try it

Open the Verify branch with its plus button and watch the other boxes slide to make room. Then close Write.

Branches that open and close

Each box is a node, and a node with children has a small plus or minus button. Closing a branch hides everything below it and the remaining boxes glide to their new places. An author can start any branch closed, as with Verify above. If the selected node is inside a branch you close, the selection moves up to the branch itself, and selecting a hidden node from elsewhere opens the branches above it.

Read nodes from a data source

A tree can also come from a flat list where each record names its parent. This one is written in YAML. Its detail pane sits outside the figure and still follows the selection.

When to reach for it

Use a tree diagram when the parent–child relationships are the point, and readers benefit from seeing the whole shape before the details. For nested data that readers inspect field by field, a data tree is more compact. Keep labels to a few words and put explanations in the detail pane.

For every reader

Tab reaches the selected node. ↑ and ↓ move through visible nodes, → opens a branch or moves to its first child, and ← closes a branch or moves to its parent. In the enlarged view, the cursor, magnifier, laser, pen, highlighter, and eraser are available. With scripts off, the authored nested list and every detail stay readable.
