Design with hierarchy

The signal ladder

Every DocForm feature pulls the eye with a different strength, from loud to quiet. This ladder ranks them so you can choose what to spend attention on.

Reading time
About 10 minutes
You will
Know how loud each feature is and when to use loud ones

Four rungs

A feature’s signal strength is how hard it pulls the eye away from everything else. The ladder has four rungs. Each feature guide names its rung in the Signal strength line near the top, and that line links back here.

RungHow it pulls the eyeHow often to use it
LoudColour and tint that jump out before anything is readRarely: about one per section
StrongLarge blocks and headings that shape the pageA few per page, each with a clear job
MediumSmall labels and marks that guide while you readFreely, wherever they help
QuietText and tools that wait until you need themEverywhere; most of a page is quiet

Some features change their rung with use. A table is strong, but shading its cells by value makes those cells loud. A glossary term you mark on purpose is medium, while the same word underlined automatically is quiet.

Loud

Loud features use colour, tint, or a box to catch the eye before anything else is read. They are the page’s alarms and highlights, and they only work while they are rare.

Strong

Strong features are the landmarks of a page: its title, its section headings, and the big blocks a reader stops to look at. They shape the page more than they decorate it.

Quiet

Quiet features are the calm background of a page: the text itself, and tools that stay out of the way until you reach for them. Most of every page should be quiet.

A sidenote is quiet as well: it waits in the margin until you want it.Sidenotes, peek bubbles, and hold-to-expand cards move detail out of the main text, so the main text can stay short.

A few features have no signal of their own because they work behind the scenes. Design tokens tune the look of every rung at once. Setup, the site and page settings, and the offline tools for search and data, explained in Behind the scenes and Offline tools, never appear on the page at all.

Spend loud signals sparingly

Think of loud signals as a small budget. Every loud element competes with every other one, so each extra one makes the rest weaker. An accent budget keeps them strong.

The budget in one line At most one callout per section, colour only when it carries a meaning, and nothing loud right beside a chart or demo.

When to spend a loud signal

  • Something must not be missed, such as a warning that prevents lost work.
  • Something needs an action, such as a button that runs a demo.
  • A meaning repeats across the page, such as one colour for one status or one chosen item highlighted in every view.

Skip loud signals for decoration, for things that are merely interesting, and for repeating what a heading already says. A sidenote or a peek bubble is usually the better home for an aside.

How much one section can carry

  • One callout per section at most. If you want a second, one of the two belongs in the text or in a sidenote.
  • Tags only where each one names a meaning the reader has learned, such as “On by default”.
  • Colours from the site’s roles, so each colour keeps the same meaning on every page.
  • Medium signals first. An eyebrow, a bold word, or a labelled field often does the job without colour.

Quiet down beside a chart or demo

A chart, animation, or interactive demo is already the strongest thing in its section, and its own colours are already loud. Let it lead.

  • Put what to notice in the caption, not in a callout next to the figure.
  • Keep one short “Try it” prompt, not several.
  • Leave tags out of the heading above the figure.
  • Move side remarks into a sidenote or a peek bubble.
