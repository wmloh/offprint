Share experiment results

Turn the results of an experiment into a page that colleagues can explore, check, and keep as a standalone copy. This recipe combines named data, intervals, results tables, scenario comparisons, TeX math, and Interactive export.

Reading time
About 10 minutes

The goal

A lab grew basil seedlings under four light conditions and measured shoot height after 14 and 21 days. The page should let a colleague see each estimate with its uncertainty, check the exact numbers, compare each treatment with the control, and take away a copy that still works on their own computer.

Illustrative numbers

The measurements on this page are made up to show the layout. They are not real results.

State the design first

Question
How does the light a seedling receives change how tall it grows?
Conditions
Control (white light, 16 hours a day), Red-enriched, Blue-enriched, and Short day (white light, 12 hours a day)
Replicates
12 seedlings per condition, one from each of 12 sowing batches
Measure
Shoot height in centimetres on day 14 and day 21

Each estimate is the mean height of 12 seedlings. Its 95% interval comes from the spread of those heights:

\bar{x} \pm t_{0.975,\,n-1}\,\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}

Here \bar{x} is the mean, s the standard deviation, and n = 12 the number of seedlings. Writing the design and the formula near the top tells readers how to interpret every number that follows.

Show each estimate with its uncertainty

Try it

Switch Harvest to Day 14 and watch the estimates move. Rest the pointer on a dot, or open Table, to read the exact mean, its interval, and the number of seedlings behind it.

A dot and interval plot keeps the estimate and its uncertainty together, so readers do not mistake a small gap between two dots for a real difference. The Harvest choice is a parameter that filters the shared named source.

Try it

Step through the variants with Next variant. Each one changes a single condition from the control. Read the paired change and its interval: an interval that stays clear of zero suggests a difference larger than the batch-to-batch noise.

A scenario comparison names the baseline, shows what each variant changed, and puts both on one scale. Because every sowing batch supplied one seedling to each condition, the page reports a paired difference, which removes variation between batches.

Hand over a standalone copy

Results often travel by email or shared drive to people who will never open the original project folder. Choose Export in the reading controls, pick the site folder, and select this page.

Interactive
Keeps the Harvest choice, chart readings, table sorting and filters, and the treatment comparison working in the saved file.
Reader
Saves the page as it looks now, which suits an archive or a printout.

Try it

Set Harvest to Day 14, then open the export dialog and build an Interactive preview. The copy starts with the choices you made on this page.

The steps and options are described in Export a standalone copy. When results come from a pipeline that reruns, ask the agent to keep the data in a named file so a refresh updates every view at once.

Features used

  • Named sources feed the dot and interval plot and the results table. A parameter chooses the harvest.
  • TeX math states how each interval is computed.
  • Scenario comparisons show each treatment against the control with a paired difference.
  • Standalone export saves an Interactive or Reader copy to share.
