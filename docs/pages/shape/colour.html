Colours, meanings, and themes

Colour roles and the legend

A role gives a colour a job. In these docs, purple labels always introduce something to try. A site can reuse one role on tags, callouts, headings, and figures that support accents. Change the role’s colour once and the items that use it follow. The legend is the key readers use to learn these meanings. Give each role a short description in the site settings, and readers see it when they rest the pointer on the legend or on a tag of that role.

Which theme opens first?

A theme sets the appearance of the page, including its background and text shades. On a first visit, OffPrint can follow your device’s light or dark setting, called the system preference. A site can also choose a light or dark starting theme. Once you use a theme button, your saved choice comes first if the browser allows the page to remember it.

When to reach for it

Keep most text in the regular text colour and give accents a consistent meaning. Start with a small set of roles that readers can learn quickly. Put a legend near the first use of the roles, and keep the same meanings on every page. Use words such as Caution as well as colour so the message survives printing in gray.

For every reader

Reach the theme button with Tab and press Enter to switch. The swatches wrap on narrow screens and keep their names in print. With scripts off, the palette still follows the device’s appearance and the legend above has a text version.
