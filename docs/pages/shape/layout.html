Section lines and split views

Each main section gets a thin coloured line in the margin, so readers can see where it starts and ends. Split views place two blocks side by side, such as a picture beside its explanation.

How the layout works

Section lines

Look along the left margin beside the main headings on this page. Each line runs from a main heading to the end of its section. Smaller headings, like the one above, belong to the same section and share its line. In focus mode the line stays sharp even when nearby text softens.

The default sequence is blue, green, purple, orange, pink, and brown, then blue again. This page uses the site’s tip role on the next main heading to show a single override. That heading still takes its place in the sequence, so the section after it gets orange.

Split views

Shares describe how the available width is divided after the gap. Two to one gives the first pane twice as much room as the second. Vertical centring lines up the middle of each pane, which helps when one is much shorter. With scroll reveals enabled, the panes enter one after another each time the split nears the middle of the screen. That entrance can be turned off for individual splits.

When to reach for it

Use section lines on long pages where readers need to see where one idea ends and the next begins. Give a heading its own role colour when the words also explain what the reader should notice.

Use a split when readers need to see two related blocks together, such as a diagram and its explanation. Keep both panes short enough to take in at a glance, and give extra width to the pane that needs it.

For every reader

On a narrow screen the panes stack in reading order. The numbered section rail takes priority if it overlaps the section lines. In print the lines shorten to marks beside headings and the panes print fully visible. With scripts off, the section marks stay at heading height and the panes stay visible in equal columns or stack on a narrow screen.
