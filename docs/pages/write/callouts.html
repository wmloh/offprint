Callouts

A callout is a tinted box that pulls one important note out of the flow of text. Its icon, title, and colour tell readers what kind of note it is.

A tip in motion

Try it

Scroll slowly to move the box through the middle of the screen. If it’s already visible, scroll past it and then come back to see it slide in from the right.

This box is a callout. Its slide comes from the optional Scroll reveals feature. The box stays visible near the start or end of a page where there’s less room to scroll.

A stationary caution

Try it

Compare this caution with the tip above. The title and icon give you a clue before you read the body. The tint comes from a colour role, a named meaning such as tip or caution that can be reused across the site.

The scripts-off look

Try it

Look for the thin border and quiet background on the plain box. With scripts off, the boxes still show their text and any authored icons and titles. A colour assigned through a site role needs scripts.

When to reach for it

Use a callout for a tip, caution, or decision someone might miss while scanning. Give it room to stand out. One callout in a section is usually enough, especially beside a figure or chart.

For every reader

Use a title or opening words that carry the meaning in either theme, in print, and at phone width. Print shows the box in place. Choose a stationary callout when readers need to refer back to it while they scroll.
