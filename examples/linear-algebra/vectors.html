Vectors

Definition A vector in \R^2 is an ordered pair of real numbers, written as a column:
\vec v = \begin{pmatrix} v_1 \\ v_2 \end{pmatrix}.
We draw it as an arrow from the origin to the point (v_1, v_2).

Adding vectors

Add vectors component by component. In the picture, place the tail of \vec v at the tip of \vec u; the sum runs from the start of the first to the end of the second.

\vec u + \vec v = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}.

Scaling vectors

Length and the dot product

The length of a vector comes from the Pythagorean theorem, \norm{\vec v} = \sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2}. The dot product combines two vectors into a number that measures how much they point the same way:

\vec u \cdot \vec v = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 = \norm{\vec u}\,\norm{\vec v}\cos\theta.

Take \norm{\vec u} = 3 and \norm{\vec v} = 2, and change the angle between them:

\vec u \cdot \vec v

At this angle the vectors make an angle.

Cauchy–Schwarz inequality For all vectors, |\vec u \cdot \vec v| \le \norm{\vec u}\,\norm{\vec v}, with equality exactly when one is a multiple of the other.
Proof in the plane

From the angle form, |\vec u \cdot \vec v| = \norm{\vec u}\,\norm{\vec v}\,|\cos\theta|, and |\cos\theta| \le 1. Equality needs \theta = 0^\circ or 180^\circ, so the arrows lie on one line.
