Pump monitoring with PCA

A cooling-loop pump already has four sensors. Use its healthy operating history to decide how many principal components to keep in a reconstruction-based fault screen.

Decision rule Start with the smallest model that retains at least 90% of baseline variance. Check what it flags on six held-out windows before recommending a setting.

What the sensors record

Each row pairs the mean flow, discharge pressure, vibration RMS, and bearing temperature measured over the same five-minute window.RMS summarizes vibration amplitude over a window. The example starts with these window summaries.

The smooth drop in the flow trace represents a scheduled load change. A healthy pump can move through a wide range of readings as demand changes.

How the synthetic run was designed

Four smooth, orthogonal operating traces drive the baseline. The first moves all sensors together. The second pairs flow and pressure against vibration and temperature. The two smaller traces give the model some baseline variation in the remaining directions. The baseline recipe regenerates all 32 windows.

What came from field measurements?

All readings and scenario labels are synthetic. The units, time-window structure, and need to keep evaluation separate from fitting reflect a typical monitoring workflow.
